# Polaris

**Real-time probability estimation for Polymarket's 5-minute crypto up/down markets.**

Polaris ingests Binance trades, aggregates them into per-second window features, and estimates
`P(up)` at every second of each 5-minute window. Sweeping that estimate across the (price-delta × elapsed-time) plane
yields a probability **surface** — a heatmap/contour of the binary option's fair value, with the current window tracing
a live path across it. Comparing
`P(up)` to Polymarket's implied price (the YES quote) is the trading **edge**.

The estimator is pluggable (a `ProbabilityModel` protocol): a Bayesian mixed-kernel model and a gradient-boosted
(LightGBM) challenger both ship today, with room for others — all judged head to head on the same block-CV Brier. They
land close to each other, which is itself a finding: the estimator was never the bottleneck. **The label and the
target were** — see the status note below.

> ## Conclusion (2026-10-08): no tradeable edge with the data and latency available here
>
> The project asked one question: **is it mathematically feasible to beat Polymarket's 5-minute
> crypto Up/Down markets?** After fixing the label, finding the exact settlement rule, building
> free real-time feeds and testing every strategy we could find or think of, the answer is **no**,
> for anyone without sub-second, two-legged execution.
>
> **How these markets work (measured, not assumed).** A window settles Up iff Chainlink's
> TWAP-60 at its last second ≥ its value at its first (99.79% agreement with Polymarket's own
> settlement over 2,421 windows); TWAP-60 is the trailing-60s mean of Chainlink spot to within
> 0.1 bp. 15-minute and 4-hour markets use the same stream. Takers pay `0.07·p·(1−p)` per share
> (1.75¢ at 50¢); makers pay nothing and get 20% of it back.
>
> | Strategy (all after fees, real settlement) | Result |
> |---|---|
> | Out-forecast the market (KDE / GBM on Binance features) | still behind it: Brier gap −0.005…−0.008, t≈−4 |
> | Live paper trading, original and improved models | −15.0% (z −5.6) and −14.9% (z −3.4) return on capital |
> | Late-window taking on the settling TWAP (live Chainlink + live book) | −5.4¢/share (z −2.2); Binance-led −2.7¢ (z −2.9) |
> | Market making, naive or model-selected (millisecond replay) | ≤ break-even even at the head of the queue with 50 ms reaction |
> | Favourite–longshot bias | loses in every price bucket |
> | Endgame sweep (asks > 0.99) | break-even, no depth |
> | YES + NO < $1 in one market | 0.03% of snapshots |
> | 5m / 15m cross-series pairs (logically guaranteed ≥ $1) | **riskless** in 3.6% of windows — but median life 2 s, ~8 shares, ~$0.40 each; gone at 1 s latency |
>
> The last row is the only genuine inefficiency found, and it is the kind the literature
> describes: combinatorial, fleeting, and taken by bots competing in milliseconds.
>
> **What is worth keeping.** The settlement rule and how to compute it from free data; the
> label pipeline that trains on real settlements by default; the fee-aware backtest and paper
> report; the free Chainlink/CLOB streaming collectors with stall watchdogs; and a long list of
> data traps written up in [`RESEARCH_LOG.md`](RESEARCH_LOG.md) (sessions 21–35). No order has
> ever been placed; there is no execution path in this codebase.

> ## Earlier status (2026-09-16): the strategy loses money; the label was wrong
>
> **Polaris was trained and scored on the wrong outcome for two months.** Polymarket states the
> rule in each market's own description — which nobody had read:
>
> > resolve to "Up" if the **time-weighted average price (TWAP)** of Bitcoin, **generated by
> > Chainlink**, of the time range specified in the title is greater than or equal to the price at
> > the beginning of that range.
> > *Resolution source: `data.chain.link/streams/btc-usd-twap-60s-streams`*
>
> `window_observations.outcome` compared the last **Binance** trade price at window end against the
> last before window start. That is wrong on **two independent axes**: wrong *source* (Chainlink,
> not Binance) and wrong *functional form* (an average over the window, not an endpoint
> comparison). Polymarket switched to this rule on **2026-08-07**; our label agreed 95.8% before
> the switch and only **88%** after.
>
> ### What it cost
>
> Grading the market against our own label made it look far worse than it is. Re-scored on real
> settlement, the sign of the headline result **flips**:
>
> | graded on | model | market | gap |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | our label | 0.181 | 0.201 | +0.025 |
> | **actual settlement** | 0.187 | **0.159** | **−0.021** |
>
> The live forward test flips with it: **1,553 gated trades at real ask prices give a 34.0% win
> rate, z = −4.41, return on capital −12.7%** — robust to leaving out any single day. Worse, on the
> *bad* label the same trades score z = +2.53. The wrong label was not adding noise; it was
> manufacturing an increasingly convincing false positive as the sample grew.
>
> ### What we found out
>
> Roughly **three quarters of the deficit was structural, not informational.** A TWAP is *partially
> observed* as a window runs — by t seconds in, t/300 of the final average is already fixed. The
> model was predicting "end price vs start price", a near-martingale, while the contract pays on
> "running average vs anchor". Four features describing the contract's actual geometry — chiefly
> `req_bps`, how much the remaining seconds must average to flip the sign — cut the gap to the
> market by **70–77%**:
>
> | | BTC gate | ETH gate |
> |---|---|---|
> | production features | −0.0188 | −0.0216 |
> | **TWAP geometry** | **−0.0065** | **−0.0049** |
>
> Those four features beat the six production ones outright, and they help the **KDE** more than
> the GBM (0.026–0.034 vs 0.017–0.021) because they *replace* dimensions rather than add them.
>
> ### Where it stands
>
> The remaining ~0.005–0.008 gap is still **negative and significant** (t ≈ −4). The likely last
> cause: we still reconstruct the TWAP and its anchor from Binance instead of reading the Chainlink
> series the contract settles on. `chainlink-collect` now records that series (free, forward-only —
> Chainlink's own history API is paid, from $150/mo), and the test needs a couple of weeks of data.
>
> **None of this is a route to profit.** Closing the gap entirely buys *parity* with the market, and
> a trade needs an edge on top of parity that then clears the ~1.1¢ spread. Every correction in this
> project's history has moved the model toward the market, never past it.
>
> **Guardrails now in the code, not just in this note:** `poly_resolutions` stores real settlements;
> `polaris backfill-resolutions` runs hourly and logs the disagreement rate every pass;
> `load_dataset` trains on settlements **by default** and refuses to silently fall back to the
> proxy; `polaris paper-report` re-scores the live test on demand. No order has ever been placed;
> there is no execution path in this codebase.
>
> <details><summary>Superseded: the 2026-09-11 price retraction</summary>
>
> Backtest prices came from `/prices-history` (1-minute resolution) forward-filled to per-second,
> leaving them a mean 12.9¢ from the real book inside the trade gate. That retraction stands, but is
> now secondary: the label beneath it was also wrong.
> </details>
>
> See [`WHITEPAPER.md`](WHITEPAPER.md) and [`RESEARCH_LOG.md`](RESEARCH_LOG.md).

## Pipeline

```
Binance WS / Vision ─► crypto_trades ─► crypto_1s_bars ─► window_observations ─► model ─► P(up)
     (ingest)             (aggregate)       (aggregate; features incl. TWAP geometry)      │
                                                                                            ▼
Polymarket CLOB (book, fills) ─────────────────────────────────────────────► edge = P(up) − price − fee
Polymarket Gamma ─► poly_resolutions  (REAL settlements — the training label)
Chainlink via Polymarket relay ─► chainlink_spot / chainlink_twap  (the series markets settle on)
```

  core/        config (pydantic-settings) · db (psycopg3 pool) · schema.sql · prune (retention)
  ingest/      Binance WS → PostgreSQL (live) · Binance Vision backfill

  chainlink/   Chainlink TWAP-60 collector (free relay, forward-only)

### How markets settle (measured)

A window resolves **Up iff Chainlink's BTC/USD TWAP-60 at the window's last second ≥ its value at the first**
(99.79% agreement with Polymarket's own settlement over 2,421 windows). TWAP-60 is the trailing-60-second mean of
Chainlink spot, so the outcome is the **average price over the final minute vs. the average over the minute before the
window opened**. The same rule and stream settle the 15-minute and 4-hour markets. Polymarket switched to this rule on
2026-08-07; before that, a Binance end-vs-start proxy agreed 95.8% of the time, after it only 88%.

Training uses **real settlements** (`poly_resolutions`) by default — `load_dataset(label_source="settlement")`. The
Binance-derived `window_observations.outcome` is a proxy kept for reference only.

### Fees

Takers pay `0.07 · p · (1 − p)` per share (1.75¢ at p = 0.5); makers pay nothing and receive 20% of the market's taker
fees. The fee is applied in the backtest, the paper report and the live trade gate (`TAKER_FEE_RATE`).

### Design decisions (full list in RESEARCH_LOG.md)

- **No Kafka.** Binance WS writes straight to PostgreSQL, idempotent via `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`.
- **psycopg3** throughout (async + COPY).
- **Pydantic where it pays, not on the hot path** — config and API models; ingested trades are plain tuples.
- **Pluggable model** behind a `ProbabilityModel` protocol, judged on leave-one-**window**-out (block) Brier: ~300
  per-second rows share one label, so the effective sample size is the window count.
- **One feature pipeline.** The aggregator stores features once; training, live inference and the dashboard read the
  same columns.
- **Forward-only data is protected.** Order books, Chainlink series and live signals cannot be re-fetched, so `prune`
  refuses to delete them.

## Quickstart

Requires [`uv`](https://docs.astral.sh/uv/) and Docker.

```bash
docker compose up -d                       # PostgreSQL
uv sync                                    # deps (add --extra mlx for Apple-Silicon calibration)
cp .env.example .env                       # defaults match docker-compose

uv run python -m polaris.main backfill             --days 14   # Binance trades
uv run python -m polaris.main backfill-resolutions --days 30   # real Polymarket settlements (the label)

uv run python -m polaris.main ingest       # live Binance → PG      (separate processes)
uv run python -m polaris.main aggregate    # trades → bars → features
uv run python -m polaris.main serve        # API on :8000;  cd frontend && npm run dev  for the dashboard
```

`docker compose --profile app up -d` runs all of it as containers, plus the forward-only collectors (`book-collect`,
`chainlink-collect`, `resolutions`, `paper-trade`, `late-paper`); dashboard at `localhost:5174`.

All as `python -m polaris.main <command>`. Configuration lives in `.env` (see `.env.example`).

## The model

Two estimators ship behind the `ProbabilityModel` protocol, selected by `MODEL_KIND`:

- **`kde_twap` (default)** — a product-kernel Beta-Binomial KDE over the contract's own geometry: price vs. the
  settlement anchor, the running TWAP, the distance to strike, and the fraction of the final average already locked.
  Tuned, it reduces to **price vs. anchor × locked fraction**; the other two dimensions are carried but near-flat.
- **`kde`** — the original six-feature KDE (delta, elapsed time, momentum, order-flow imbalance, volatility,
  time-of-day). Kept as a baseline.
- **`gbm`** — a heavily-regularised LightGBM control.

Every KDE weights each training observation by a product of per-feature kernels and turns the weights into a
Beta-Binomial posterior — `P(up) = (α₀ + Σwⱼyⱼ) / (α₀ + β₀ + Σwⱼ)`, with `n_eff = Σwⱼ` as the confidence measure and
a 95% credible interval from the Beta. Bandwidths are tuned by Optuna under block cross-validation. The derivations
are in [`WHITEPAPER.md`](WHITEPAPER.md) §3–5.

**How good is it?** Against real settlement, `kde_twap` scores Brier ≈ 0.139 (old `kde` ≈ 0.156; base rate 0.25) —
and still trails Polymarket's own prices by 0.005–0.008 (*t* ≈ −4). It forecasts well; the market forecasts better.

### Edge & gate

```
edge_yes = P(up)     − yes_price − fee(yes_price)
edge_no  = (1−P(up)) − no_price  − fee(no_price)        fee(p) = 0.07·p·(1−p)
```

A trade is signalled when the better edge ≥ `min_edge`, `n_eff ≥ min_n_eff`, and elapsed time is inside the trade
window. It is a readout only — nothing in this codebase places orders.

## Frontend

A React + Vite dashboard lives under `frontend/`, built on Tailwind around the probability surface as the centerpiece:
the heatmap — conditioned on the live window's actual momentum/ imbalance/volatility/time-of-day, not just delta and
elapsed time — with the current window's live path riding across it, a signal band (`P(up)` vs market, edge, trade gate,
95% CI,
`n_eff`), and supporting panels (live order flow, feature state, Polymarket market + edge, diagnostics). It consumes
`api/`'s WebSocket (`/ws/{symbol}` →
`tick`/`matrix`/`micro`/`macro`/`polymarket`) and REST (`/api/*`).

With `kde_twap`, the heatmap's vertical axis is price vs. the TWAP anchor (the coordinate the model uses), and the
Diagnostics panel shows whichever bandwidths the serving model has. Still deferred: dedicated backtest and
calibration panels.
